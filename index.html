Reference · nutrition principles
◎
The one rule: Never try anything new on race day or the night before. Rehearse your pre-run breakfast during training so you know it sits well.

Fats & veg
  • Carbs are fuel: oats, rice, potatoes, whole-grain bread, fruit — don't fear them on run days.
  • Protein repairs: aim ~1.2–1.6 g per kg bodyweight (eggs, dairy, legumes, chicken, fish, tofu).
  • Colour every meal: vegetables and fruit for micronutrients and recovery.

→ Around your runs

  • 1–2 hrs before: a light carb snack — banana, toast with honey, a small bowl of oats. Avoid heavy or high-fat food.
  • Short easy runs: fine to go fasted if that suits you; keep water handy.
  • Within 30–60 min after: carbs + protein to recover — yoghurt & fruit, eggs on toast, a milk-based smoothie.
  • Longer sessions (W6–7): a few sips of water mid-run; nothing fancy needed under 5K.

◇ Hydration

  • Daily target: ~2–3 litres of water; more on hot days and hard sessions.
  • Check colour: pale straw = well hydrated. Dark = drink up.
  • Electrolytes: after sweaty runs, a pinch of salt in food or an electrolyte drink helps.
  • Limit: alcohol the night before key runs — it wrecks sleep and hydration.

★ Race-week fuelling

  • Mon–Thu: eat normally, stay hydrated, nothing experimental.
  • Fri (day before): slightly more carbs across the day (not one giant meal), plenty of fluids.
  • Race morning: your rehearsed breakfast 2–3 hrs before — e.g. oats + banana + coffee if you're used to it.
  • Right after: celebrate, then refuel with carbs + protein within the hour.

Sleep & recovery

Log last night's sleep. The tracker watches your rolling average and debt, then recalibrates tonight's target.

Reference · recovery principles
☾
Target 7–9 hours a night, and protect the two nights before hard runs and race day. If race-eve nerves cost you sleep, don't panic — the night before that one matters most.

☾ Sleep foundation

  • Consistent times: same bed/wake window, even weekends — it stabilises energy.
  • Wind down: screens off ~45 min before bed; dim lights, cool dark room.
  • Caffeine cutoff: none after ~2 pm so it doesn't fragment sleep.
  • Bank sleep: in weeks 6–7, add 30 min a night as mileage peaks.

✚ Active recovery

  • Rest days are training: take them fully — walking is fine, hard efforts are not.
  • Mobility: 5–10 min of hips, calves, hamstrings after runs; foam-roll tight spots.
  • Legs up: a few minutes with legs against the wall after long runs eases heavy legs.
  • Listen for pain: soreness is fine; sharp or one-sided joint pain means back off.

⚑ Injury-proofing

  • Warm up: 3–5 min brisk walk + leg swings before every run.
  • Cool down: easy walk + light stretch; never stop dead from a hard effort.
  • Shoes: run in proper, non-worn trainers; replace if they're flat or painful.
  • 10% rule: the plan already ramps gently — don't add extra hard mileage on top.

◈ Strength & core

  • Twice a week (20 min): squats, lunges, glute bridges, calf raises, planks.
  • Why it matters: stronger hips and core = better form and far fewer injuries.
  • Keep it light: bodyweight or light weights; leave energy for your runs.

Race day — 24 October

You've done the work. This is the fun part. Here's how to run it smart and enjoy the finish.

◷ Pacing plan

  • Start slower than feels right — adrenaline makes everyone go out too fast.
  • First km easy, settle into the pace you trained at.
  • Middle km steady, breathing controlled — able to say a few words.
  • Last km: if you feel good, lift the pace and finish strong.
  • Walk breaks are allowed — a run/walk finish is still a finish.

☼ Morning timeline

  • Wake ~3 hrs before the start.
  • Breakfast 2–3 hrs before — your rehearsed meal, sip water.
  • Arrive early: toilet, bib, easy walk + dynamic warm-up.
  • Line up honestly by expected pace; don't get swept along.
